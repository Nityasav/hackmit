# Demo and pitch

## Opening pitch

Schools can employ large finance teams and still struggle to connect payroll, purchases, grant restrictions, and supporting evidence. SchoolTrace gives them a coordinated team of financial investigators: specialists follow the transactions, an auditor challenges their claims, and a lead investigator turns reviewed evidence into actions before external audit fieldwork.

The payoff is earlier correction and clearer accountability. Do not promise guaranteed funding, fraud prevention, or a clean external audit.

## Five-minute demonstration

### 0:00–0:35 — The mystery

Open fictional Maplebridge's September dashboard. Ask: “Enrollment fell 6%. Why did payroll rise, and why is the student-support grant nearly exhausted?” Show that the dataset includes payroll, invoices, bank records, award terms, and service evidence. Point out the management accounting profile and coverage status.

### 0:35–1:30 — Specialists follow different paths

The payroll analyst builds a variance bridge. Legitimate substitute coverage explains part of the increase. The transaction detective clears two similar invoices because their delivery records differ. The restricted-funds specialist finds a salary charged entirely to an award, with incomplete allocation support.

Click the graph path from payroll line to award to governing term to source page. Display actual tool events and evidence references, not prewritten agent chatter.

### 1:30–2:15 — The auditor refuses to guess

The specialist initially suspects an allocation problem. The auditor requires current service evidence before quantifying a confirmed finding. Fulfill the in-app request by adding the synthetic service allocation record. It supports a 60/40 split for a $10,000 salary.

The system calculates a $4,000 reclassification. Explain that this is a change in funding allocation, not recovered cash or proof of theft.

### 2:15–3:10 — One decision, consistent consequences

Open the proposed journal, evidence, and before/after effects. Approve the simulation. Show department and award spending change while institution-wide payroll and cash remain unchanged. If the fixture includes an overstated reimbursement receivable, show its separate reviewed proposal and effect.

The graph invalidates old report claims and the application publishes a new consistent snapshot. Open the final finding's action owner and supporting workpaper.

### 3:10–4:15 — Memory earns its place

Show the reviewed September precedent and its dates/scope. Open October: one recurring case uses it to avoid a repeated clarification. A new contract changes the allocation basis, so agents reject the old precedent for that case and consult current evidence.

Show the logged action changed by memory. This is more persuasive than merely saying the system remembers.

### 4:15–5:00 — Measured results

Show the paired October comparison with identical documents, books, model, and budgets. Report actual precision/recall, false positives, unsupported claims, consistency, human interventions, and costs. Include the sample size and any failures. End with the reviewed action report and clickable evidence.

## Demo prerequisites

- Fresh seed/reset succeeds; source and ledger hashes recorded.
- One live investigation tested on current code and configured provider.
- A missing-evidence document can be added without exposing hidden labels.
- Human review visibly changes only the simulation.
- Month-two comparison saved with complete manifests.
- No placeholder metric presented as a measured result.
- Saved replay available and clearly labeled if network/model service fails.

## Questions judges may ask

**Why multiple agents?** They have different questions, tools, and review duties. Demonstrate one specialist's result changing another's task and an independent re-performance that changes a conclusion.

**Why a context graph?** It identifies applicable relationships, connects claims to evidence, scopes precedents by time/entity, and determines which outputs become stale when a source changes.

**Does this eliminate hallucinations?** No. Exact calculations, evidence requirements, counterevidence, independent review, and human gates reduce and expose unsupported conclusions. Show the unsupported-claim metric.

**How is this different from a workflow script?** Accounting constraints are deterministic, but agents choose hypotheses, searches, handoffs, and follow-up evidence requests based on the documents and reviewer feedback.

**Why schools?** Restricted funding, shared staff, student-service obligations, and fragmented procurement create cross-system investigations where correctness matters. Institutions still need qualified finance staff and external auditors.

**Can this handle a university?** The architecture allows another institutional profile, but the demonstrated build supports one school district. University grant, tuition, endowment, and accounting policies require additional adapters and validation.
